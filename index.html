The Moon · 1967

Surveyor 3

A robotic visitor whose lunar landing site was later visited by astronauts.

The mission

Surveyor 3 landed on the Moon in April 1967 and studied the surface with a camera and a soil-sampling instrument.

The unexpected return

In 1969, Apollo 12 astronauts visited the landing area and retrieved parts of Surveyor 3 for examination on Earth.

Its legacy

The encounter connected a robotic mission with human exploration and gave scientists an opportunity to examine hardware exposed to the lunar environment.

Source: NASA — Surveyor 3 [1]

The Moon · 1964

Ranger 7

A camera-carrying spacecraft that sent close-up lunar images before impact.

The mission

Ranger 7 was designed to photograph the lunar surface as it approached the Moon.

The final images

In July 1964, it returned thousands of images before striking the lunar surface. Those pictures helped reveal details that telescopes on Earth could not show clearly.

Its legacy

The spacecraft did not survive as a functioning machine, but the images it transmitted helped prepare the way for later lunar exploration.

Source: NASA — Ranger 7 [2]

The Moon · 1971

Apollo 15

The mission that introduced a crewed lunar rover to explore farther from the landing site.

The mission

Apollo 15 carried the first Lunar Roving Vehicle used by astronauts on the Moon. It allowed the crew to travel farther and collect samples across a wider area.

The equipment left behind

The rover was not designed to return to Earth. It remained on the Moon when the astronauts departed.

Its legacy

The rover expanded the reach of human fieldwork on the lunar surface and helped astronauts investigate the landscape more efficiently.

Source: NASA — Apollo 15 [3]

Mars · 2018

InSight

A stationary lander that listened for marsquakes to investigate the planet's interior.

The mission

NASA's InSight lander studied the inside of Mars using instruments that measured ground motion and gathered information about the planet's interior.

Why it stopped

Dust gradually accumulated on its solar panels, reducing the power available to its instruments. NASA ended the mission in December 2022 after the lander could no longer communicate reliably.

Its legacy

Its measurements revealed important information about marsquakes and the internal structure of a rocky planet beyond Earth.

Source: NASA — InSight [4]

🛞
Mars · 1997

Pathfinder & Sojourner

One landing mission, one tiny rover, and a new way to explore the Martian surface.

The mission

Mars Pathfinder landed in July 1997 and deployed Sojourner, a small rover that examined nearby rocks and tested technologies for robotic surface exploration.

Two connected legacies

The lander demonstrated a landing approach using airbags, while Sojourner showed that a rover could move around and investigate the terrain on Mars.

Why remember both?

The lander and rover had different jobs, but their achievements belong to one mission story. Together, they helped shape later Mars exploration.

Source: NASA — Mars Pathfinder [5]

🚁
Mars · 2021–2024

Ingenuity

An experimental helicopter that proved powered, controlled flight was possible on another planet.

The experiment

Ingenuity travelled to Mars with the Perseverance rover. It was designed as a technology demonstration, not as a science rover.

Beyond the original plan

After its first successful flight in April 2021, it completed many more flights, helping demonstrate how aerial exploration could support future missions.

The ending—and the legacy

Ingenuity's flight campaign ended in January 2024 after rotor damage prevented further flights. Its achievement opened new possibilities for exploring worlds with thin atmospheres.

Source: NASA — Ingenuity [6]

These machines did not all stop working for the same reason. Some completed their planned missions; others reached an operational limit or were lost. Their stories help us understand both exploration and engineering.
